Particle System with Sorted and Unsorted Pools
I built a particle system, the engine feature behind effects like fire, smoke, and sparks, designed to handle thousands of short-lived particles without allocating memory every frame.
  • Wrote a free-list allocator that pre-reserves a fixed block of particles up front. Spawning and killing a particle just moves it between lists instead of asking the OS for memory, which avoids the stutter that constant allocation would cause.
  • Split particles into two pools, a sorted pool of live particles that get drawn in order and an unsorted pool of free slots ready to reuse, so the hot path of updating and rendering only ever touches the particles that are actually alive.
  • Built an emitter that spawns particles with configurable behavior: gravity pulling them down over their lifetime, and color interpolation so they smoothly fade or shift color as they age.
Matrix Transformations and Rendering
I implemented the matrix math that powers 3D rendering, the transformations that take a model's local coordinates and figure out where they end up on your screen.
  • Wrote the core linear algebra by hand: matrix multiplication, inversion, and the world/view/projection transforms, rather than leaning on a library, so I understood exactly what each step was doing to the geometry.
  • Rendered 3D objects through DirectX 11 with a movable camera, feeding the transformation matrices to the GPU each frame so the scene updates as the camera moves.
  • Added a grid floor as a visual reference, which made it easy to see and debug whether the transformations and camera movement were actually behaving correctly.
